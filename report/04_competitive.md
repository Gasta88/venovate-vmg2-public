# Competitive

Four uniqueness claims were tested against the recorded competitor landscape:
two hold outright, two hold only if narrowed to their venom-specific slices.
Every named player below is recorded in the competitor inventory with its own
source URL and check date (80 rows, 18 role categories; row counts and columns
are documented in the Methods annex).

### P6 G.E.M. Screening Service

The inventory added the model-supplier axis for P6: murine in-vivo CROs

December 2024–November 2027 [@nc3rs2024gem; @whoantivenom2017].

## P5 VenoMate and P3 Marketplace: overlap check

Both packages serve academic labs, pharma and biotech, and the AI-bio
frontier. The package definitions record the overlap on both sides and keep
the value propositions distinct: VenoMate is the self-service digital layer —
members post offers and needs, rule-based matching ranks results, contact via
introduction requests only. P3 is the high-touch brokered layer — consultancy,
strategic advisory, collaborative research, industry-academia partnerships.

P5 VenoMate feeds P3 Marketplace: self-service discovery escalates into
brokered work. That is a funnel, not cannibalisation. P3 and P4 do
conflict: both list synthesis, folding and purification. The routing rule:
disulfide-rich peptide requests go to P4 first; P3 brokers only what P4
cannot do.

The ResearchGate and LinkedIn caveat applies here too: those platforms
threaten both packages at once as free alternatives, but they are not two
distinct competitors.

## P3 Marketplace and P4 Peptide Service: overlap check

The marketplace lists synthesis, folding and purification — the entire P4
offering. The conflict is real, not presentational: the same buyer could
source peptide work through brokering or directly from P4. The routing rule
above is the recommended coordination: disulfide-rich requests go to P4
first, and P3 brokers only what P4 cannot do. Pricing conflict is avoided by
the same rule; P4 sets the tariff, P3 marks up brokered external work.

## VenoMate monetisation

The package description is explicit about the platform's limits: profiles add a
badge and analytics but no ranking advantage, and contact runs through
introduction requests only, with no direct data exchange. The revenue
streams remain paid profiles (€400 facilities, €2,000 industry), referral
fees of 3–5% on work routed to any package, and white-label licensing —
and all of them accrue to the host institution, not to a central platform
(see § Revenue-capture risk).

## Uniqueness-claim tests

Four packages carry "only" or "no comparable" claims in the package
description. Each gets exactly one verdict, tested against the 80-row
inventory.

- **P1 VENOSPATIAL** claims MSI-central positioning. Verdict: holds only if
  narrowed to bundling mass-spectrometry imaging with venom and toxin
  expertise. Instrument vendors and service providers exist in depth; none in
  the inventory pairs them with this field.
- **P2 In Silico Workshop** claims a computational-training gap. Verdict:
  holds only if narrowed to venom-toxin specialisation. Free and low-cost
  training is abundant; none of it is venom-specific.
- **P5 VenoMate** claims expert and resource discovery for the venom field.
  Verdict: the claim holds. No platform we found records which venom groups
  hold which samples, instruments or capacity. The closest is the
  International Society on Toxinology's members' directory, which lists
  contacts only and costs about USD 55 a year [@ist]. Whether facilities will
  pay €400 for visibility on top of that is untested (V5).
- **P6 G.E.M.** claims *Galleria* antivenom screening as a service. Verdict:
  holds. Supplier and model rows exist; no *Galleria*-based screening service
  was found. The pre-screen positioning in the Market section states where the
  service sits during validation; it does not narrow the claim tested here.

## Advantages

Each claim ties to recorded evidence.

- **Venom-specific breadth across a multi-country network.** No incumbent
  covers both the expert-discovery layer (IST, e-COST, VIVO) and the
  workflow and tooling layer. The geographic breadth is evidenced by the
  publication and funding corpora behind P5 and P3 (Annex 1).
- **COST credibility.** The volunteer network's participation in the COST
  framework gives institutional credibility individual consultancies cannot
  claim; the mechanics are publicly documented (https://www.cost.eu/cost-actions/participate-in-an-ongoing-action/).
- **Regulatory navigation.** Navigating access and benefit-sharing
  obligations under the Nagoya Protocol for the network's sourcing partners
  is a competence competitors do not default to [@nagoya]; it is a
  sourcing-side service, not an entry barrier protecting the broker,
  matching or training lines.
- **Disulfide-rich peptide know-how.** The density of disulfide-specialist
  rows in the inventory (Creative Peptides, Concept Life Sciences, IRBM, Alta
  Bioscience, AnaSpec, Senn Chemicals, Sai Life Sciences, GL Biochem) signals
  a real, brokerable capability.

## Sustainability risk: the COST horizon

The volunteer network underpinning VENOVATE's early credibility participates
in the COST Action framework. The mechanics — working-group membership,
directory access, national contacts — are publicly documented. The VMG grant
runs to October 2026 (grant letter, ref. E-COST-GRANT-IG19144-289974d6
[@grantletter]), as the package lead confirms. What the
network needs beyond that date is a formal governance structure that survives
the voluntary phase and a host institution providing administrative and legal
continuity; neither is evidenced yet, and both are open items below.

## Revenue-capture risk (VMG4)

Package revenue lands at each lead's institution, not at a platform level.
Only VenoMate has platform-level streams — paid profiles, referral fees,
white-label licensing — and even those accrue to the host institution.
Individual packages may survive and thrive at their lead
institutions while the coordinating platform barely persists without its own
revenue base. This is flagged for the VMG4 business-model spec, not resolved
here.
